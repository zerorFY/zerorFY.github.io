AI product discovery

AI product discovery begins when a buyer describes a problem rather than naming a familiar brand. A useful publishing page therefore explains the situation a product addresses, the people it serves, and the evidence a reader can verify. Clear nouns matter because retrieval systems connect entities, needs, and sources through language. Concrete details also help a human decide whether a recommendation fits. A page that only repeats broad marketing claims may be easy to read yet difficult to distinguish from hundreds of similar pages.

Good discovery content keeps its primary facts visible in ordinary HTML. It gives each page a stable address, a precise title, and a consistent publisher identity. Supporting prose should add context without hiding the central answer. Comparisons are most credible when their criteria are explicit, while claims are strongest when the originating page can be cited directly. Discovery is not the same as endorsement: an engine may find a document without ranking it, quoting it, or trusting it. Measuring those stages separately makes a publishing experiment easier to interpret and improves future decisions.
