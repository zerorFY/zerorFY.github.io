Generative engine citation

Generative engine citation allows a reader to move from a synthesized answer back to its supporting evidence. The best citation points to the exact document, preserves the publisher identity, and accompanies a claim that the page actually supports. A clear question-and-answer block may help retrieval systems isolate a requested fact, especially when the surrounding article uses the same terms consistently. The structure should remain useful to people rather than becoming a pile of hidden keywords or repetitive fragments.

Citation quality has several dimensions. An answer may reproduce the correct fact but omit its source, name the right domain but link to the wrong page, or cite an original page while paraphrasing the fact incorrectly. Each result reveals a different failure. Controlled phrases make exactness easy to score, and canonical URLs make provenance easy to inspect. Testing should begin in a fresh session without supplying the URL, because direct retrieval bypasses discovery. The researcher can then repeat the task with the URL to confirm whether the remaining challenge is finding the page or understanding its content.
